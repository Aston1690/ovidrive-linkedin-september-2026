Fleet is next

PNG · 1200 × 1200Editable SVGCaption
Read supplied caption

FinOps stopped being a cloud story a while ago. The 2026 State of FinOps report just confirmed it. Ninety per cent of FinOps teams now manage SaaS spend, sixty four percent manage licensing, fifty-seven per cent manage private cloud, forty eight percent manage data centre. One practitioner summed up the shift better than I could: first they asked us to fix cloud, then fix the software mess, now fix the contract and licence mess. Meanwhile seventy two percent of organisations still blew through their cloud budget last year, even with all that discipline applied. Cloud got its own financial discipline because the spend was large, fragmented across teams, and nobody owned it end to end. That description fits fleet just as well as it fits cloud. Multiple suppliers. Multiple countries. Spend that shows up on a dozen invoices before anyone adds it up properly. FinOps did not stay inside cloud because cloud was special. It spread because the problem it solves exists everywhere spend is large and nobody owns it properly. Fleet is next. #FleetFinOps #FleetManagement #FleetTechnology #CostManagement

Week 1 · Thursday

Procurement and AI priorities

PNG · 1200 × 1200Editable SVGCaption
Read supplied caption

Procurement's 2026 workload is going up 8%. Headcount is going down. Budgets are shrinking too. The Hackett Group's 2026 Procurement Key Issues Study puts real numbers on it: workload up 8%, headcount down 0.9%, operating budgets down 0.4%. The only number moving the other way is technology spend, up 6.1%, because it has to. For the first time, deploying AI-enabled technology has made it into procurement's top three priorities. Supply continuity has also overtaken cost reduction as the top objective this year, driven by the kind of geopolitical disruption most teams were not planning around twelve months ago. Put those together and procurement is not being asked to do more with less. It is being asked to do more, with less, under more external pressure, and to automate its way there. That forces an actual choice about where the automation goes first. The obvious candidates are the spend categories procurement already loses the most hours to, multiple suppliers, multiple contracts, numbers that are supposed to match and often do not. Fleet sits squarely in that category for a lot of procurement teams, and it rarely gets prioritised first, mostly because nobody has done the work of showing what leaving it alone is actually costing. #Procurement #FleetFinOps #SupplyChain #ProcurementAI

Week 2 · Tuesday

Where fleet software growth is coming from

PNG · 1080 × 1350Editable SVGCaption
Read supplied caption

The fleet management software market is worth 11.61 billion US dollars this year, and Fortune Business Insights expects that to reach 47.81 billion by 2034. Growth of 19.4% a year, for a market most people still associate with dots moving on a map. That association is already out of date. The fastest growing segment inside that number is not tracking. It is AI and advanced analytics, expanding at 23.4% a year, faster than the market around it. Electric fleet management is close behind, not for the vehicles themselves but for the software managing battery life, charging costs and energy forecasting that petrol fleets never needed. Electric van sales alone were up 40% in a single year. GPS told you where a vehicle was. None of that tells you what it cost, why it cost that, or what to do differently next time. That is the layer we built OviDrive around. Not where the vehicle is. What the fleet is actually costing, and what is driving that cost up or down. The market is growing because the question fleet teams are asking has changed. Software that only answers the old question is going to feel very dated, very quickly. #FleetManagement #FleetFinOps #FleetTechnology #AI

Week 2 · Thursday

Cost management and CFO risk

PNG · 1200 × 1200Editable SVGCaption
Read supplied caption

Cost management just jumped from third to first on the list of what keeps CFOs up at night. Deloitte's Q1 2026 CFO Signals survey polled 200 CFOs at North American organisations with at least a billion dollars in annual revenue. Fifty two percent now name cost management their most worrying internal risk, the top response. Six months earlier it ranked third, at forty seven percent. That is a North American finding, but the pressure behind it is not regional. Rising technology investment, thinner margins and more scrutiny on spend are not unique to one market. The same survey found forty nine percent citing pressure to invest in new technology, cloud and AI, as a driver of that cost concern, almost level with shrinking margins at forty eight percent. Fleet sits inside that concern more often than it gets credit for. It is one of the largest line items most of these organisations carry, split across suppliers, contracts and countries, and it rarely gets the same financial scrutiny as headcount or capital spend. If cost management has genuinely become the top internal risk on CFOs' own list, the categories still running on spreadsheets and supplier portals are exactly where that risk is hiding. #CFO #FleetFinOps #CostManagement #FinanceLeadership

Week 3 · Tuesday

An agent is only as useful as its data

PNG · 1080 × 1350Editable SVGCaption
Read supplied caption

By the end of 2026, forty percent of enterprise applications will run task specific AI agents. A year earlier, in 2025, it was under five percent. Gartner published that forecast in August 2025, and it has held up as one of the sharpest single year jumps in enterprise software history, faster than cloud adoption, faster than mobile first design, faster than the first wave of generative AI chatbots. The interesting part is not the agents themselves. It is what has to exist before an agent is actually useful. An agent can only act on what it can see clearly. If a fleet still has vehicle records in one spreadsheet, supplier invoices in an inbox, and contract terms in a portal nobody fully trusts, adding an agent on top does not fix that. It just automates the confusion faster. Every business function is about to find out whether its own data was ever organised enough to hand to something that acts on its behalf, not just something that answers questions about it. Fleet is one of the functions that has not had that conversation yet. #AI #FleetFinOps #FleetTechnology #EnterpriseAI

Week 3 · Thursday

Same number. Opposite outcome.

PNG · 1200 × 1200Editable SVGCaption
Read supplied caption

Everyone is quoting the same Gartner number this year: forty percent of enterprise apps will run task specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from under five percent in 2025. Fewer people are quoting the other Gartner number. By the end of 2027, Gartner expects more than forty percent of agentic AI projects to be cancelled outright. Not paused. Cancelled. The reason is not that the technology does not work. Gartner points to escalating costs, unclear business value and weak governance, and to a market where a lot of what gets called agentic is really a chatbot or an old automation tool with a new label. Gartner's own estimate is that only a few hundred of the thousands of vendors claiming agentic capability actually have it. So the honest version of the AI story this year is not adoption speed. It is that speed without the groundwork is exactly how you end up in the forty percent that gets cancelled. An agent making decisions about your fleet still needs the same thing a person does to make a good decision. Accurate contract data. Cost data that reconciles. One version of what is true, not three. Move fast on the agent. Move faster on making sure it has something real to work from. #AI #FleetFinOps #AgenticAI #FleetTechnology
