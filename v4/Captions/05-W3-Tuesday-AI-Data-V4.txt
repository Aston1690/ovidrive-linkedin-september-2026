By the end of 2026, forty percent of enterprise applications will run task specific AI agents. A year earlier, in 2025, it was under five percent.
Gartner published that forecast in August 2025, and it has held up as one of the sharpest single year jumps in enterprise software history, faster than cloud adoption, faster than mobile first design, faster than the first wave of generative AI chatbots.
The interesting part is not the agents themselves. It is what has to exist before an agent is actually useful.
An agent can only act on what it can see clearly. If a fleet still has vehicle records in one spreadsheet, supplier invoices in an inbox, and contract terms in a portal nobody fully trusts, adding an agent on top does not fix that. It just automates the confusion faster.
Every business function is about to find out whether its own data was ever organised enough to hand to something that acts on its behalf, not just something that answers questions about it.
Fleet is one of the functions that has not had that conversation yet.
